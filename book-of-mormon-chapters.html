    How I made this

    The idea is simple. Every person, divine being and named group the text refers to is a circle. If a chapter refers to someone, that person is in the chapter. That’s it. A line between two people doesn’t mean they met. It means the same chapter refers to both of them.

    There are 272 circles in all. To get there I had to make some choices. Here they are, so you can disagree with them.

    • “The Lord” and “God” count as Jesus Christ. So does every title. God the Father is counted separately, and only when the text says “Father.” The Holy Ghost is counted separately too.
    • A mention counts even in passing. “The sons of Mosiah” counts Mosiah. “Since Lehi left Jerusalem” counts Lehi.
    • Places and peoples don’t count. “The land of Nephi” and “the people of Nephi” are not Nephi. The Nephites and Lamanites as nations are not on the map.
    • Groups of specific people do count. The sons of Mosiah, the twelve disciples, the Three Nephites, Helaman’s 2,000.
    • Shared names are separate people. There are four Nephis, two Almas and three Helamans. I told them apart by book and chapter.
    • People without a name are included when the text makes clear who they are. The brother of Jared. Lamoni’s father. Mary. All unnamed angels share one circle.
    • Chapter headings are not part of the text and were not used.

    What does this miss? If a chapter only says “he” or “the king” and never the name, the person is usually not counted. That matters for the five chapters with no gold square. They are chapters of escape, war and murder. I can’t say the Savior is absent from them. I can only say the words I searched for are not there.
