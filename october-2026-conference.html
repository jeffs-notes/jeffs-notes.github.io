Draw Closer to Jesus Christ

What the prophets, seers, and revelators taught, how their messages connect, and where to begin if you want to take the conference to heart.

An analysis of the sixteen messages given by the First Presidency and the Quorum of the Twelve Apostles, drawn from the November 2026 Liahona. Prepared November 2026.

Purpose

Each general conference brings a set of messages from the First Presidency and the Quorum of the Twelve Apostles. Sixteen were given in October 2026. Heard one at a time over two days, they can feel like sixteen separate sermons. Read together, they turn out to be a few teachings spoken many times, from many directions, by men who prepared independently of one another.

This page is a map of those teachings. It names the themes that run across the sixteen talks, shows where the Brethren reinforced one another, gives each speaker's own words on each theme, and suggests where to start for anyone who wants to go back to the talks with a plan. It is meant for any member who already knows what general conference is and would like to see the whole of it at once.

The central message

Fifteen of the sixteen messages pointed to the same place: come closer to Jesus Christ. The Brethren did not teach trust, love, covenants, courage, or testimony as separate programs. Each was framed as a way of drawing nearer to the Savior, and every other theme in this analysis is connected to that one more strongly than to any other.

Beneath that, three teachings stood out for how many of the Brethren reached for them independently: God's promises are sure and no faithful person is excluded from them; those who are drifting or doubting are to be loved with truth and patience, not argued with or abandoned; and covenants, sealed by the Holy Spirit of Promise, are what bind us to Him and to each other.

How this analysis was done

  1. Read all sixteen talks in the November 2026 Liahona: the three members of the First Presidency (President Oaks twice, including his closing remarks), and the thirteen members of the Quorum of the Twelve who spoke.
  2. Named each talk's core topic, then looked for the themes that appeared in more than one talk. Ten themes emerged. A theme had to be taught by at least four of the Brethren to be counted.
  3. Coded every talk against every theme as a major emphasis, a passing mention, or absent. This matrix is what the connection views below are drawn from.
  4. Collected a representative quotation from each speaker under each theme, with its Liahona page number. Every quotation was checked word for word against the source text, and each secondhand quotation (an apostle quoting a prior Church leader) is labeled as such.
  5. Traced shared scriptures, stories, and quotations across talks, since two speakers reaching for the same passage independently is a signal worth teaching from.

The theme list and the coding are one reader's judgment, offered as a study aid. The quotations are exact; the groupings are interpretive.

The ten themes

Ordered roughly by how many of the Brethren taught them. Each theme opens with a short answer to the question what is this about?, then names the one or two talks to listen to first, then gives what each speaker actually said. Many talks appear under several themes; that overlap is the connection.

1

Jesus Christ Is the Way — Draw Closer to Him

This is the conference's center of gravity. The point is not a new doctrine but a direction: whatever a person is carrying—doubt, grief, a wayward child, a trial that won't lift—the answer is to move toward the Savior, not to solve the problem first and come to Him later. President Oaks said it plainly: He is the way, and strong faith "means daily trying, one step at a time." Elder Kearon gave the picture of rising air: the Savior is the lift, and we just have to keep turning into it. The theme carries a built-in warning, too—drift is gradual, so closeness has to be chosen again every day.

The unifying message of the conference (and the Liahona cover: "President Oaks: 'Draw Closer to Jesus Christ'"). Nearly every speaker framed his message as a way to come closer to the Savior.

2

Trust the Lord's Promises — Especially in Trials

This theme speaks to members who have done what they were asked and are still waiting. The teaching is that God's promises are sure, that He keeps them on His timetable and often through other people, and that the trial itself is not evidence He has forgotten. President Eyring taught that the promise is not for removal of the hard thing but for strength in it. Elder Renlund warned against fixating on today's "cold oatmeal" and forgetting what God has already done. Elder Bednar added the hardest case: no faithful person will lose a promised blessing because of someone else's choices. Together they say: there is no asterisk next to your name.

Three talks were built almost entirely on this (Eyring, Renlund, Bednar), and several others leaned on it.

  • Elder Bednar, Sealed by the Holy Spirit of Promise p. 102
  • President Eyring, "Fear Not, I Am with Thee" p. 68

3

Love Those Who Struggle or Have Stepped Away — Truth with Love

This is about how disciples treat people who are drifting, doubting, or gone—in their own families and among the people they worship with. President Oaks named these members as a major concern of the prophets and said the Lord is "relentless in His loving pursuit" of them. Elder Rasband told grieving parents to stop lecturing and start loving, and to hand the burden to Christ. Elder Gilbert gave the balance: standing for truth without charity loses the Spirit, but softening God's laws to seem loving separates us from the One who loves them most. The theme is a posture—arms open, standards intact, patience long.

This may be the most personally urgent thread. President Oaks named it as a "major source of concern" to the prophets, and Elders Rasband and Gilbert each devoted a full talk to it.

4

Covenants, Ordinances, and the Holy Spirit of Promise

This theme explains what covenants actually do and why they must be kept, not just made. Elder Bednar called ordinances and covenants our "credentials" for returning to God's presence, and taught that the Holy Ghost, as the Holy Spirit of Promise, ratifies them over time as we stay faithful—like a passport that must be kept in good condition to be honored. Elder Gong taught the same doctrine from the temple side and urged a "personal temple culture" of regular attendance, family names, and gratitude. President Oaks pointed to weekly sacrament and temple attendance as simple protections. With the Salt Lake Temple opening to the public in April 2027, it is a timely teaching to sit with.

Two apostles (Gong and Bednar) taught the same doctrine from D&C 132:7 in the same conference—a strong signal. President Oaks's closing remarks then pointed the whole Church toward the Salt Lake Temple.

  • Elder Gong, Come to the Lord in His Holy House p. 5

5

Unity and Peacemaking — Avoiding Contention and Coarse Speech

This theme is about how Latter-day Saints speak to and about each other, especially online. President Uchtdorf taught that Christ's whole ministry broke down walls, that Satan's work is to divide, and that what unites the Saints is not politics or culture but covenants and love for God and neighbor: "If we are not one, we are not His." Elder Cook called coarse, cruel, or reputation-destroying communication reprehensible and said it drives away the Holy Ghost. Elder Gilbert added the practical skill of resisting the impulse to respond in a heated moment. Unity here is not agreement on everything; it is charity that refuses contention.

Where to start

  • President Uchtdorf, Christ-Centered Unity p. 94
  • Elder Cook, Integrity and Virtue Lead to Happiness p. 80

  • President Uchtdorf p. 95

    "If we are not one, we are not His."

  • Elder Gilbert p. 119

    "There are times when being a peacemaker means that we resist the impulse to respond and instead, with dignity, remain quiet" (quoting Elder Andersen).

  • Elder Cook p. 82

    "What we are seeing in society and social media today is conduct and language that are often coarse and crude and lack virtue. . . . Any use of the internet to bully, destroy a reputation, or place a person in a bad light is reprehensible."

    "In a world of commotion, contention, and cares, the Lord invites us to a place of spiritual safety, harmony, and holiness—the house of the Lord."

6

Bear Witness and Share the Gospel — "Love, Share, and Invite"

This theme says that testimony is a gift given to be passed on, and that the gathering of Israel is accelerating and needs ordinary members' voices. Elder Caussé taught that a testimony need not be eloquent—"it only needs to be true"—and that saying "I know" is itself an act of love. President Oaks observed that many in the world are hungry for the Savior and called on members to share what they know. Elder Stevenson reduced it to three verbs for children and adults alike: love, share, invite. The emphasis is on simple, frequent, natural witness rather than programs or arguments.

7

Faith, Courage, and the Daily Practices That Sustain Them

This theme deals with the quiet mechanics of staying faithful in a noisy world. Elder Andersen taught that faith produces courage and courage feeds faith, and that both need stillness—hard to find with a phone always in hand and "artificial information" flooding in. Elder Kearon warned that in gliding, if you are not climbing you are descending, and spiritual altitude is lost the same way: by neglecting prayer, scripture, and communion with the Spirit. President Oaks said strong faith requires more than strong desire; it is daily trying. The teaching is that discipleship is sustained by small, repeated, deliberate acts.

Several speakers warned about the quiet drift that comes from neglecting simple habits, and about the noise of our phones.

Where to start

8

Integrity, Virtue, and Wise Choices — "Where Will This Lead?"

This theme concerns personal conduct and its consequences, for ourselves and for those watching us. Elder Cook taught that integrity, virtue, and happiness are linked by design, and that repentance has to be a daily habit rather than an occasional event. President Christofferson applied the principle to gambling, now in everyone's pocket: it is morally wrong because it takes without giving, it crowds out worthier things, and even "harmless" wagering can lead a weaker brother or sister astray. The recurring question is President Oaks's: where will this lead, and what is its opportunity cost? Virtue is framed not as restriction but as the road to joy.

Where to start

  • Elder Cook, Integrity and Virtue Lead to Happiness p. 80

9

Our Identity as Children of God — and His Grace Is for All

This theme establishes who we are and whom the gospel is for. Elder Stevenson told children that being a child of a loving Heavenly Father is "your most important identity," and that knowing it makes us both stronger and kinder. President Oaks used the Book of Mormon to refute the idea that grace saves only a chosen few: Christ invites all and "denieth none." Elder Renlund taught that the Atonement's "ample provision" reaches every human circumstance. President Uchtdorf framed the Church as the great family of God being gathered. The theme answers both "Am I included?" and "Are they?" with yes.

Where to start

  • Elder Stevenson, Fruit to Make One Happy p. 113
  • President Oaks, The Gospel of Jesus Christ Is for All p. 89

What they said

  • President Oaks p. 91

  • Elder Renlund p. 33

    "'Ample provision' means that all human circumstances are within the Savior's redemptive reach. . . . There is more than enough to cover any breach, need, or deficit for all of us, for each of us."

  • Elder Bednar p. 102

    "Life is a homeward journey for all of us, back to the presence of God" (quoting President Packer).

10

Ministering — Bearing One Another's Burdens

This theme is about how God keeps His promises through people. President Eyring taught that the Lord usually meets our needs through another person and thanked members for being the answer to someone's prayer—often by acting on a name that came to mind. President Uchtdorf tied it to the baptismal covenant: we agreed to mourn with those who mourn. Elder Soares showed that fasting and generous fast offerings are ministering in another form, turning our hearts outward to "break every yoke." Elder Gilbert illustrated it by listening for two hours before teaching. The teaching is that succor is a shared work: Christ's promise, our hands.

Shared scriptures, stories, and quotations

Places where two or more of the Brethren reached for the same passage or the same prophetic statement. They are worth noticing because they show the Spirit working across independent preparation, and they make natural starting points for family or personal study.

  1. Holy Spirit of Promise (D&C 132:7) — Elder Gong made it his third doctrinal foundation (p. 5); Elder Bednar made it the center of his entire talk (p. 102–104). Both also addressed the hard question of a sealing where one spouse is unfaithful: Gong—"God will not require a woman or man to be sealed or live in an eternal relationship he or she does not choose"; Bednar—"Can the unrighteous exercise of moral agency by another person rob me of what God has promised? The answer to this question is an emphatic no!"
  2. Alma the Younger remembering his father's words (Alma 36:17) — Elder Caussé used it to show how a remembered testimony becomes "the spark of hope" (p. 12); Elder Rasband used it to show the power of a parent's persistent prayers for a wayward child (p. 59–61). Together: your testimony, borne now, may be what a loved one remembers later.
  3. Mosiah 18:8–9 (bear one another's burdens / stand as witnesses) — President Eyring (p. 69), President Uchtdorf (p. 94), and Elder Caussé (p. 11) all quoted the baptismal covenant from Alma's sermon at the waters of Mormon.
  4. Weekly sacrament as protection — President Oaks (p. 90), President Uchtdorf (p. 95), and Elder Andersen (p. 74) each pointed to the weekly renewal of covenants as a simple, protective practice. Elder Kearon quoted President Oaks's BYU counsel on the same point in his notes (p. 53).
  5. Lehi's dream and the tree of life — Elder Rasband (the mist of darkness and the great and spacious building, p. 59), Elder Stevenson (the fruit "desirable to make one happy," p. 113), and Elder Gilbert ("Stay at the tree . . . eating the fruit is a happy thing," p. 120).
  6. President Oaks's April 2026 talk "Alive in Christ" — quoted by Elder Rasband (p. 59), Elder Cook (p. 82), and Elder Gilbert (p. 119–120) on peacemaking and on standing for truth without surrendering values.
  7. President Oaks's "Where will this lead?" — President Christofferson built his gambling message on President Oaks's "opportunity cost" framing (p. 27–28); Elder Andersen quoted President Oaks's own turning-point decision to accept a demanding calling (p. 74).
  8. Faith → courage → faith — Elder Andersen's thesis ("faith strengthened his courage, and his decision brought increasing faith") is echoed in Elder Gilbert's closing: "It takes courage to stand for truth. It takes even more courage to stand for truth with love."
  9. Happiness and joy as the fruit of discipleship — Elder Cook (integrity → happiness), Elder Stevenson ("fruit to make one happy"), Elder Renlund ("joy in our here and now"), Elder Gong ("happy and forever"), and Elder Kearon ("soar in the joy of the Lord").
  10. President Nelson's teachings carried forward — "Peacemakers Needed" (Cook, Gilbert), "the covenant path" (Oaks, Cook), "spiritual momentum" (Uchtdorf), "Hear Him" (Gilbert), and the gathering as "the greatest work on earth" (Uchtdorf).

Five teachings to carry forward

If you take only a few things from this conference, these five carry its weight. They are the teachings the most Brethren returned to, and each has a talk or two that will bring it back to mind.

  1. Draw closer to Jesus Christ; He is the way. (Oaks, Kearon, Rasband, Uchtdorf, Renlund) The simplest message, and the one every other theme hangs on. Elder Kearon's picture of lift and sink gives it a vocabulary you can use day to day.
  2. Trust His promises; there is no asterisk. (Eyring, Renlund, Bednar, Andersen) For anyone carrying a long trial, a broken sealing, or a prayer that has gone unanswered. Three apostles in one conference promised that the blessings will come.
  3. Love those who struggle, and hold to truth with love. (Oaks, Rasband, Gilbert, Caussé) President Oaks named those of diminishing faith as a major concern of the prophets. Elder Rasband speaks to grieving parents and friends; Elder Gilbert gives a five-step pattern for anyone who has to say something hard to someone they love.
  4. Covenants are credentials; the Holy Spirit of Promise seals them. (Gong, Bednar, Uchtdorf, Oaks) Two apostles taught Doctrine and Covenants 132:7 in the same conference, and the Salt Lake Temple opens to the public in April 2027. This is a year to think about what our covenants are for.
  5. Be a peaceable, united people, online and in person. (Uchtdorf, Cook, Gilbert, Rasband) Elder Cook's warning about coarse communication and President Uchtdorf's "if we are not one, we are not His" speak directly to how we treat one another, in the pews and on our phones.

One narrower teaching deserves its own mention, especially for parents and for youth and young adults: President Christofferson's warning on gambling and sports betting in "O Be Wise." It is unusual for a member of the First Presidency to spend a full conference address on a single behavior; the figures he cited on boys aged 11 to 17 and on young adult women explain why he did.
